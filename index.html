Featured case study · 2026

Decarbonising an already-green plant: Acciaieria Arvedi, Cremona

Arvedi's Cremona EAF steelworks was the first steelmaker to hold a third-party-certified net-zero claim. I set that reported figure aside and rebuilt the physical Scope 1 baseline from the plant's EU ETS verified emissions. I then decomposed it by source and built a costed 2030 abatement package.

What the analysis found

  1. The net-zero claim rests on one physical result and three accounting choices. The 138 kg CO₂/t intensity is real. Scope 2 is zeroed on a market basis, the residual is offset with forestry credits, and Scope 3 is excluded.
  2. Absolute emissions are near a record high. Verified Scope 1 has risen roughly fifteen-fold since 2005, and the plant is net short 82,536 allowances.
  3. A physically real 2030 pathway exists, and it is affordable. It clears the Mission Possible Partnership steel trajectory at under 2% of product value.
  4. 59% of it depends on one measure. Biocarbon substitution needs about 36,400 t/yr of certified biochar, against a European market measured in tens of kilotonnes. Securing that supply is the constraint that decides the rest.

Capex and opex are modelled engineering estimates from public sources, not supplier quotes. The Scope 1 baseline is the only third-party-verified input.

MACC · 2026

Abatement cost analysis for a mid-market EAF steel plant

I modelled eight interventions for a 500,000 t/yr plant against a 150,000 tCO₂e baseline at an 8% discount rate. I also ran sensitivity on EU allowance prices to find the carbon price at which each capital-intensive measure turns viable.

~6,000 tCO₂e/yr of negative-cost abatement found, for €2.7M capex
